Job Market Paper

Loss Aversion and Agricultural Investment: Experimental Evidence from Smallholder Farmers in Kenya

with S. Janzen, B. Kramer, and K. Shikuku

Abstract
Smallholder farmers persistently underinvest in productivity-enhancing agricultural inputs. We study whether loss aversion explains this gap. We combine a dynamic model of fertilizer decisions under Cumulative Prospect Theory with a lab-in-the-field experiment involving 209 maize farmers in Kenya, individually eliciting loss aversion parameters via price list lotteries and maximum likelihood estimation. Loss aversion is associated with 1.4 fewer kilograms of fertilizer per unit, 14 percent of the sample mean. This implies that loss aversion operates as an independent barrier to investment, beyond the risk and liquidity constraints that financial products are designed to address. We introduce four decision-making environments to better understand how loss aversion interacts with fertilizer investment when farmers have access to index insurance, emergency credit, and drought-tolerant seed technology. We find that the negative association between loss aversion and investment does not weaken when either product is available, and that loss aversion reduces willingness to pay for insurance and emergency credit by similar amounts, pointing to the certain upfront payment structure as the common barrier rather than features specific to either contract.
